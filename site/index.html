    How it was done

    Built so the numbers can be trusted.

    01

    Audit the data

    All 5,880 images were checked. One byte-identical pair with conflicting gaze labels was found and removed before any modelling.

    02

    Split by person

    Five folds by identity. Tests prove no person is ever on both sides, and every method sees the same test images.

    03

    Share every sample

    For each fold, seed and budget, one set of training images is drawn and given to every method, nested from 5 to ALL.

    04

    Commit the plan first

    Classifier settings, threshold, prompts, comparisons and Study 2 were committed to git before they ran. Later additions are labelled post-hoc.

    05

    Honest intervals

    95 % intervals resample whole people, not images, with the same draws for every method so differences are paired.

    06

    Rerun and compare

    The full study was rerun from a clean commit. All 3,160 records matched to the last digit.

    What this cannot tell you

    • Not eye contact. People looked at a lens or a dot in a lab, not at someone talking to them.
    • One lab. 56 people, 2 m away, a chin rest, head turns only left and right.
    • Unequal prior knowledge. L2CS-Net was trained with gaze labels elsewhere, so its budget undercounts.
    • No fairness claim. The dataset has no demographic labels, so subgroup performance is unknown.
    • One face, one target. Real conversations have several people, a moving gaze target and speaking turns. None of that is tested here.
    • Not for monitoring. These are research models. Do not use them to watch people.
